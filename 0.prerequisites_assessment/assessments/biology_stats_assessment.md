## Answer Key

### Part 1: Biology Fundamentals

1. **A) DNA → RNA → Protein** — The Central Dogma states that DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is then translated into protein. Genetic information flows in this one-way direction (with exceptions like retroviruses).

2. **B) RNA is a messenger/intermediate; it carries genetic information from DNA to ribosomes for protein synthesis** — RNA serves as the intermediate that transfers the genetic code from DNA in the nucleus to the protein-synthesis machinery in the cytoplasm.

3. **Acceptable answer:** Gene expression is the process by which information from a gene is used to produce a functional product, typically a protein. It involves transcription (DNA → RNA) and translation (RNA → protein). Gene expression can be regulated so that different genes are turned on or off in different cells or conditions.

4. **B) They express different subsets of genes from the same genome** — All somatic cells in an organism have the same DNA; differentiation results from differential gene expression, not different genomes.

5. **C) DNA is copied into RNA (mRNA)** — Transcription is the synthesis of RNA from a DNA template. Translation converts mRNA into protein.

6. **Acceptable answer:** The genome is the complete set of DNA (all genes) in an organism. The transcriptome is the set of all RNA molecules (transcripts) expressed in a cell or tissue at a given time—it represents which genes are active and at what levels.

7. **B) Measure which genes are expressed and at what levels in individual cells** — scRNA-seq captures the transcriptome of individual cells, revealing cell-to-cell variation and enabling identification of cell types and states.

8. **B) mRNA carries the genetic code for protein synthesis; rRNA and tRNA have structural/catalytic roles in translation** — mRNA carries the coding sequence; rRNA forms the ribosome; tRNA delivers amino acids. All participate in translation but with different roles.

9. **Acceptable answer:** Cells share the same DNA but differ in which genes are turned on (expressed) or off. This is controlled by regulatory mechanisms (transcription factors, epigenetics, cell signaling) that vary by cell type, developmental stage, and environment.

10. **B) Are expressed in most or all cell types to maintain basic cellular functions** — Housekeeping genes encode essential functions (e.g., metabolic enzymes, cytoskeleton) and are constitutively expressed across cell types.

### Part 2: Statistics Fundamentals

11. **B) The data is likely right-skewed (a few high values pulling the mean up)** — When mean > median > mode, high values pull the mean upward, characteristic of right-skewed distributions.

12. **Acceptable answer:** Standard deviation measures the spread or variability of data around the mean. A larger standard deviation means data points are more spread out; a smaller one means they cluster closer to the mean.

13. **B) If the null hypothesis were true, there is a 3% probability of observing the data (or more extreme)** — The p-value is the probability of obtaining the observed result (or more extreme) assuming the null hypothesis is true. It is not the probability the null is true.

14. **B) The mean, median, and mode are equal** — In a normal distribution, these three measures of central tendency coincide, and the distribution is symmetric and bell-shaped.

15. **Acceptable answer:** Correlation measures association between two variables but does not prove that one causes the other. A third variable, confounding factors, or coincidence could explain the relationship. Causation requires experimental evidence.

16. **B) The increased probability of false positives (Type I errors) when many tests are performed** — With many tests, the chance of at least one false positive rises. If α = 0.05 and you run 100 tests, you expect ~5 false positives by chance alone.

17. **Acceptable answer:** FDR is the expected proportion of significant results that are false positives. In genomics, when testing thousands of genes, FDR (e.g., via Benjamini-Hochberg) controls the rate of false discoveries among all declared positives, which is more interpretable than per-comparison error rates.

18. **A) Most genes have low expression; a few genes have very high expression** — A right tail indicates a right-skewed distribution: most values are low, with a minority of genes showing high expression (common in RNA-seq data).

19. **B) We reject the null hypothesis when the probability of the observed result under the null is less than 5%** — At α = 0.05, we reject the null if p < 0.05, meaning the observed result would occur by chance < 5% of the time if the null were true.

20. **B) We reject a true null hypothesis (false positive)** — Type I error = false positive = rejecting the null when it's true. Type II error = false negative = failing to reject a false null.
